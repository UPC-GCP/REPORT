\section{2D Navier-Stokes Model}~\label{chapNavierStokes}

The Navier-Stokes equations~\cite{Shames2003} describe the motion of viscous incompressible fluids and form the foundation of computational fluid dynamics. Unlike the scalar transport problems of Chapters~\ref{chapDiffusion} and~\ref{chapConvection}, where a single PDE governs a single unknown, the NS system is a coupled set of equations involving velocity, pressure and temperature.

Two fundamental challenges distinguish this problem from the preceding chapters. The first is \textbf{pressure-velocity coupling}: in incompressible flow, pressure does not have its own time-derivative equation. Instead, it acts as a Lagrange multiplier that enforces the continuity constraint $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0$, and its value at each time step must be determined implicitly from that constraint rather than marched forward independently. The second is the \textbf{nonlinear convective term} $\rho(\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v}$ in the momentum equation, which couples the velocity components to each other and prevents the direct application of the linear solvers developed in the previous chapters.

Both challenges are addressed in combination. The \textbf{Adams-Bashforth} scheme evaluates the convective and diffusive terms explicitly using values from the current and previous time steps, so that the right-hand side of the momentum equation is fully known without inner iteration. The \textbf{Fractional Step Method}~\cite{Ferziger2002} then decouples the pressure from the velocity by splitting each time step into a predictor–Poisson–corrector sequence, reducing the coupled system to a set of independent linear problems of the type already established in the previous chapters.

The governing equations for incompressible viscous flow are:

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSMass}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v} = 0
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSMomentum}
    \rho\,\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}}{\partial t} + \rho\,(\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v} = -\nabla P + \mu\,\nabla^2 \mathbf{v}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSEnergy}
    \rho c_p\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + \rho c_p\,\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{v}\,T) = \nabla \cdot (\lambda\,\nabla T) + \dot{q}_V
\end{equation}

Equations~\eqref{eqNSMass} and~\eqref{eqNSMomentum} govern the velocity and pressure fields in all test cases. Equation~\eqref{eqNSEnergy} is the energy transport equation, which shares the same structure as the general transport equation of Chapter~\ref{chapIntroduction} with $\phi \equiv T$ and $\Gamma \equiv \lambda$; it is only activated for the Differentially Heated Cavity (Section~\ref{secDHC}), where temperature differences drive the flow through buoyancy.

The time discretization of Eq.~\eqref{eqNSMomentum} uses the \textbf{Adams-Bashforth} method~\cite{Ferziger2002}, a second-order explicit multistep scheme. The key idea is to approximate the combined convective-diffusive operator $R(\mathbf{v})$ at the unknown time level $n+1$ by linear extrapolation from the two preceding known levels $n$ and $n-1$:

\begin{equation}\label{eqRv}
    R(\mathbf{v}) = \mu\,\nabla^2 \mathbf{v} - \rho\,(\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eqAdamsBashforth}
    \rho\,\frac{\mathbf{v}^{n+1} - \mathbf{v}^n}{\Delta t} = -\nabla P^{n+1} + \frac{3}{2}\,R(\mathbf{v}^n) - \frac{1}{2}\,R(\mathbf{v}^{n-1})
\end{equation}

The coefficients $3/2$ and $-1/2$ follow from a second-order backward extrapolation of $R$ using the two known levels; this maintains temporal second-order accuracy while keeping the entire right-hand side (except for $\nabla P^{n+1}$) explicit in known quantities. The spatial discretization of $R(\mathbf{v})$ reuses the framework of the previous chapters: the diffusive term $\mu\,\nabla^2\mathbf{v}$ is discretized as in Chapter~\ref{chapDiffusion}, and the convective term $\rho\,(\mathbf{v} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{v}$ uses Patankar's scheme with the selected $A(|Pe|)$ function from Chapter~\ref{chapConvection}. The unknown pressure $P^{n+1}$ is handled by the Fractional Step Method described in the following subsection. Table~\ref{tabConfigNS} shows the changes done to the configuration structure for this model, which reformulat the definition of initial conditions and boundary conditions for each variable. In addittion, a new probe type was introduced (\textbf{Field}) to target the velocity field directly.

\subsection{Fractional Step Method}~\label{secFSM}

The Fractional Step Method resolves the pressure-velocity coupling in Eq.~\eqref{eqAdamsBashforth} by decomposing each time step into three sequential operations. The core observation is that if the pressure gradient is temporarily omitted from the momentum update, the resulting \textit{predictor} velocity $\mathbf{v}^*$ will not in general satisfy the continuity equation; the mismatch between $\mathbf{v}^*$ and the correct $\mathbf{v}^{n+1}$ is then used to derive a scalar Poisson equation for $P^{n+1}$, whose solution closes the system.

\textbf{Step 1 — Predictor.} The velocity is advanced from $\mathbf{v}^n$ to the intermediate field $\mathbf{v}^*$ using the Adams-Bashforth evaluation of $R$, with the pressure gradient omitted:

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSM1}
    \mathbf{v}^* = \mathbf{v}^n + \frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\left[\frac{3}{2}\,R(\mathbf{v}^n) - \frac{1}{2}\,R(\mathbf{v}^{n-1})\right]
\end{equation}

The field $\mathbf{v}^*$ is fully determined from known quantities and is computed explicitly without solving a linear system. At the discrete level, the $x$-momentum equation for each staggered $u$-node $P$ is written in terms of the FVM residual $\mathcal{R}^n_u$ evaluated using Patankar's unified scheme (Table~\ref{tabConvSchemes}):

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSuPredictor}
    u^*_P = u^n_P + \frac{\Delta t}{\rho\,V_P}\left[\frac{3}{2}\,\mathcal{R}^n_u - \frac{1}{2}\,\mathcal{R}^{n-1}_u\right]
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSuResidual}
    \mathcal{R}^n_u = \sum_{k \in \{w,e,s,n\}} a^n_k\,(u^n_k - u^n_P), \qquad a^n_k = D_k\,A(|Pe^n_k|) + \max(-F^n_k,\,0)
\end{equation}

where the face diffusivities, convective fluxes, and face Péclet numbers are:

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSuFace}
    D_k = \frac{\mu\,S_k}{d_{Pk}}, \qquad F^n_k = \rho\,\bar{v}^n_k\,S_k, \qquad Pe^n_k = \frac{F^n_k}{D_k}
\end{equation}

The face velocity $\bar{v}^n_k$ driving each convective flux is not stored at the faces of the $u$-control volume; it is obtained by linear interpolation between the two surrounding staggered nodes, reducing to arithmetic averaging on a uniform mesh. The $y$-momentum equation for each staggered $v$-node follows the same structure with $v$ replacing $u$ and the roles of the $x$- and $y$-directions exchanged.

\textbf{Step 2 — Pressure Poisson equation.} Subtracting Eq.~\eqref{eqFSM1} from the full update Eq.~\eqref{eqAdamsBashforth} gives the velocity correction:

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSMcorr}
    \mathbf{v}^{n+1} = \mathbf{v}^* - \frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\,\nabla P^{n+1}
\end{equation}

Taking the divergence of Eq.~\eqref{eqFSMcorr} and imposing the incompressibility constraint $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}^{n+1} = 0$ yields the pressure Poisson equation:

\begin{equation}\label{eqPoissonP}
    \nabla^2 P^{n+1} = \frac{\rho}{\Delta t}\,\nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}^*
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eqPoissonP} is a standard diffusion-type equation with the divergence of the predictor as its source term. Integrating over $V_P$ and applying the divergence theorem — following the same procedure as in Chapter~\ref{chapDiffusion} — gives:

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSM2}
    a_P P_P = a_W P_W + a_E P_E + a_S P_S + a_N P_N + b_P
\end{equation}

with coefficients $a_k = S_k / d_{Pk}$ for $k \in \{w, e, s, n\}$ (equivalent to setting $\Gamma = 1$ in Table~\ref{tabDiffCoeff}), $a_P = \sum_k a_k$, and:

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSM2rhs}
    b_P = -\frac{\rho}{\Delta t}\left(u^*_e\,S_e - u^*_w\,S_w + v^*_n\,S_n - v^*_s\,S_s\right)
\end{equation}

This is a symmetric positive-definite linear system (same structure as the diffusion equation) and is solved by BiCGSTAB, consistent with Chapter~\ref{chapConvection}.

\textbf{Step 3 — Velocity corrector.} With $P^{n+1}$ known from Step~2, the corrected velocity field is obtained from Eq.~\eqref{eqFSMcorr}. On the staggered mesh, each velocity node sits at a face between two adjacent pressure cells, so the discrete pressure gradient links immediately neighbouring pressure nodes without interpolation. For a $u$-node at the east face between pressure cells $P$ and $E$, and a $v$-node at the north face between cells $P$ and $N$:

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSM3u}
    u^{n+1}_P = u^*_P - \frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\,\frac{P^{n+1}_E - P^{n+1}_P}{\Delta x}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}\label{eqFSM3v}
    v^{n+1}_P = v^*_P - \frac{\Delta t}{\rho}\,\frac{P^{n+1}_N - P^{n+1}_P}{\Delta y}
\end{equation}

Both updates are explicit once $P^{n+1}$ is known and require no linear solve. By construction, $\mathbf{v}^{n+1}$ satisfies the continuity equation to the accuracy of the Poisson solve.

\textbf{Energy equation.} Equation~\eqref{eqNSEnergy} is discretized following the same FVM procedure as the general transport equation derived in the Introduction (Eqs.~\eqref{eqTransDisc}--\eqref{eqTransConv}), with $\phi \equiv T$ and $\Gamma \equiv \lambda$. The coefficient expressions are identical in form to those of Chapter~\ref{chapConvection} and are not reproduced here.


\subsubsection{Staggered Meshes}~\label{secStaggered}

Discretizing pressure and velocity on the same collocated grid leads to a well-known pathology. The central-difference approximation of the pressure gradient at cell $P$ in the $x$-direction is:

\begin{equation}\label{eqPgradCollocated}
    \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right|_P \approx \frac{P_E - P_W}{2\Delta x}
\end{equation}

This formula samples pressure nodes two cells apart and ignores the immediate neighbours. As a consequence, a \textit{checkerboard} pressure field (one that alternates sign between adjacent cells) produces a zero gradient at every node. The momentum equation cannot distinguish this spurious oscillatory pattern from a physically uniform pressure field, and the solver cannot suppress it.

The staggered mesh arrangement~\cite{Ferziger2002} eliminates this decoupling by relocating the velocity components to the cell faces. Pressure nodes remain at cell centres; horizontal velocity $u$ nodes are placed at the east/west face midpoints; and vertical velocity $v$ nodes are placed at the north/south face midpoints, as shown in Fig.~\ref{figStaggeredMesh}. With this layout, the pressure gradient driving the $u$-node at the east face of cell $P$ is:

\begin{equation}\label{eqPgradStaggered}
    \left.\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}\right|_e \approx \frac{P_E - P_P}{\Delta x}
\end{equation}

which links adjacent pressure nodes directly. Similarly, the discrete divergence at cell $P$:

\begin{equation}\label{eqDivStaggered}
    \nabla \cdot \mathbf{v}\big|_P \approx \frac{u_e - u_w}{\Delta x} + \frac{v_n - v_s}{\Delta y}
\end{equation}

is computed from face velocities that are the immediate neighbours of the cell centroid. Both odd-even decouplings are eliminated without any interpolation between mesh levels.

\subsection{Boundary Conditions}~\label{secNSBC}

Boundary conditions for the Navier-Stokes model must be specified separately for the velocity and for the pressure, since the two fields are governed by different equations within the FSM algorithm.

\textbf{Velocity boundary conditions.} All solid walls impose the \textbf{no-slip} condition, which sets both velocity components to zero at the wall:

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSBCNoSlip}
    u = 0, \qquad v = 0 \qquad \text{(solid wall)}
\end{equation}

For the Lid-Driven Cavity, which will be explained further below, the moving top wall prescribes a non-zero tangential velocity while maintaining zero normal velocity:

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSBCLid}
    u = U, \qquad v = 0 \qquad \text{(moving lid)}
\end{equation}

Both conditions are Dirichlet constraints and are imposed through the same coefficient modification used in Chapter~\ref{chapConvection}: the known wall velocity enters $a_P$ and $b_P$ of the corresponding momentum equation cell. On the staggered mesh, the velocity node lies exactly at the face midpoint, so no ghost-node distance correction is required for the wall-tangent component; the normal-to-wall component is zero by the no-penetration condition and is enforced directly.

\textbf{Pressure boundary conditions.} The pressure Poisson equation (Eq.~\eqref{eqPoissonP}) has no prescribed Dirichlet value at any wall: every physical boundary is solid, so $\mathbf{v}^{n+1} \cdot \hat{n} = 0$ on all wall faces. Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eqFSMcorr} in the wall-normal direction and applying the no-penetration condition yields the natural boundary condition for pressure:

\begin{equation}\label{eqNSBCPressure}
    \frac{\partial P^{n+1}}{\partial n} = 0 \qquad \text{(all walls)}
\end{equation}

A purely Neumann Poisson problem has a one-dimensional null space: $P^{n+1}$ is determined only up to an additive constant. To obtain a unique solution, the pressure is fixed to zero at a single reference node (a domain corner), which does not affect the velocity field since only $\nabla P$ enters the corrector step.
